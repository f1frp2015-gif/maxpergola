Six practical layouts

Start With the Furniture Plan

Each layout starts with a real use and the clearance, shade, drainage, or accessory decisions that come with it. Images may show optional accessories.

1. The evening lounge

Center the sectional under the louvered roof, keep any fire feature outside the required clearances, and specify dimmable lighting preparation before production.

2. The poolside kitchen

Shade the cooking line, maintain the grill manufacturer's clearances, and face the bar seating toward the pool without blocking the required pool-deck walkway.

3. The small-patio retreat

A 10 × 10 ft footprint can cover a bistro table or two lounge chairs. Keep the best view open and place a privacy screen only where exposure calls for one.

4. The outdoor dining room

Allow for the table, chair pullback, and a clear serving aisle. Align the dining area with the kitchen door, but keep the post locations out of the main traffic path.

5. The near-house pergola

A freestanding layout can sit close to the house without a wall connection. Check eaves, gutters, doors, foundation separation, and where both roof systems discharge water.

Near-house layoutFreestandingClear transition

6. The screened garden room

Use adjustable louvers overhead and add side protection only where needed. Maintain ventilation, egress, sightlines, and any local rules that apply to enclosed outdoor structures.

Site-planning checks

Account for Sun, Wind, and Roof Runoff

The view is only one part of the layout. Track seasonal sun angles, prevailing weather, runoff from the house, and where the integrated post drains can discharge without washing across a walkway or back toward the foundation.

  • Map morning and late-afternoon sun in different seasons
  • Maintain required clearances from grills, fire features, and pools
  • Keep chair pullback and walkways clear of the posts
  • Route pergola drainage away from foundations and traffic areas
  • Place screens for low-angle sun, privacy, and prevailing wind
