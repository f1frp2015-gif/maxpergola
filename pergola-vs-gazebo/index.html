Pergola vs gazebo

Pergola or gazebo? Start with the roof.

A pergola usually creates adjustable or filtered shade with a lighter, more open structure. A gazebo usually uses a fixed solid roof for continuous cover. Your weather and how you want to feel outside decide the better fit.

Compare at a glance ↓Explore pergola kits

Pergola

An open-sided structure with slats, louvers or another roof treatment. It is often selected for controllable shade, airflow and a visually lighter connection to the yard.

  • Open, architectural feel
  • Filtered or adjustable shade
  • Easy connection between zones

Gazebo

A freestanding outdoor structure that typically has a fixed, solid roof. It is often selected when continuous overhead cover and a more enclosed pavilion feeling are priorities.

  • Fixed solid roof
  • Continuous overhead cover
  • More traditional pavilion form

When a pergola is the better fit

Choose a pergola when the experience of the space should change with the day. Adjustable louvers can open for morning light and airflow, rotate for afternoon shade and close for rain protection within the product's design limits. The horizontal lines also suit modern homes and keep a stronger visual connection to the garden or pool.

  • You want to control sun instead of blocking it all day.
  • You prefer an open, architectural structure.
  • The space connects dining, cooking, pool and garden zones.
  • Integrated lighting, screens or glass options matter.

When a gazebo is the better fit

Choose a gazebo when continuous fixed cover is the main requirement and adjustable sunlight is less important. A solid roof can be a straightforward choice for a dedicated picnic pavilion, a spa shelter or a yard where a traditional roof form fits the architecture.

  • You want permanent overhead cover.
  • A fixed-roof pavilion appearance suits the property.
  • The structure will serve one stable, dedicated use.
  • You do not need the roof to open for sunlight.
Neither label guarantees performance.Wind, snow, drainage, anchoring and material durability depend on the specific product and site. Review approved documentation—not only the category name.

What about a louvered pergola?

A louvered pergola sits between the classic open pergola and a fixed solid-roof shelter. When open, the blades allow light and air. When closed, compatible systems interlock and direct rainwater into channels. It preserves the lighter pergola form while offering more weather control than fixed open rafters.

Before you buy

Ask the same hard questions either way.

01

What is it designed for?

Confirm wind and snow criteria, foundation assumptions, drainage capacity and whether documentation is available for local review.

02

What is included?

Separate the structure, roof, anchors, electrical parts, side enclosures, delivery, unloading and installation in the quote.

03

What will ownership require?

Compare cleaning, coating care, fastener inspection, moving parts, roof debris, replacement parts and warranty terms.
