Ambient light

Use low-glare illumination to make faces, tabletops, and the overall room feel comfortable.

Task light

Add focused brightness over grills, counters, serving areas, or work surfaces where visibility matters.

Accent light

Highlight posts, planting, steps, or the roofline without making every surface equally bright.

Four useful layers

Place Light Where It Solves a Job

A strong plan creates separate scenes for dinner, cleanup, conversation, and safe circulation instead of relying on one bright fixture.

1. Perimeter light for conversation

A concealed linear source around the beams can wash the seating area with soft light while keeping the center of the roof visually quiet.

Ambient layerDimmableLow glare

2. Task light over the outdoor kitchen

Put brighter, shielded light over food preparation and serving surfaces. Keep reflections and direct views of the source out of adjacent seating.

Cooking zoneShieldedSeparate control

3. Louver lighting for roof drama

Factory-prepared louver-mounted lighting can emphasize the adjustable roof pattern. Confirm wire movement, service access, and compatible components before ordering.

4. Low light for posts and paths

Use restrained light near steps, changes in level, and circulation edges. Coordinate post routing before production so field drilling is minimized.

Factory-prepared lighting

Plan the Wiring Before the Frame

Lighting added late can require visible conduit, field drilling, or inaccessible connections. A preproduction plan lets the product team review beam channels, post routes, mounting points, controls, and accessory conflicts.

  • Mark the dining, cooking, lounge, and circulation zones
  • Separate ambient, task, and accent controls where useful
  • Specify fixture and driver access for future service
  • Confirm outdoor ratings and local electrical requirements
  • Have a qualified electrician review the jobsite connection

Lighting preparation does not replace electrical design or installation. The written quote and approved documents define included components and factory work.

Common planning mistakes

Avoid Brightness Without Comfort

01

One circuit for everything

Dining, cooking, and late-night conversation need different brightness. Separate useful layers so every fixture is not on at full output.

02

Exposed glare at eye level

Shield direct views of light sources from seated positions and neighboring properties. Reflected light is often more comfortable than visible diodes.

03

Wiring decided at the jobsite

Late routing can conflict with louvers, drains, screens, or heaters. Coordinate all moving and electrical accessories before production.

Pergola lighting FAQ

Answers Before Electrical Rough-In

What lighting works best under a pergola?

Use layers: soft ambient light for the room, task light where people cook or serve, and restrained accent or path lighting where it adds depth or safer circulation.

Can LED lights be integrated into the louvers?

Some Max configurations can be prepared for louver-mounted LEDs. Confirm the plan before production so movement, wire routes, mounting, controls, and service access can be reviewed together.

Should lighting be planned before pergola assembly?

Yes. Fixture locations, circuits, controls, drivers, wire routes, and accessory conflicts should be resolved before production. Final field connections follow the approved documents and local requirements.

Who should wire outdoor pergola lights?

Use a qualified electrician where required. Outdoor components, wet-location ratings, grounding, bonding, GFCI protection, and permits depend on the jobsite and jurisdiction.
