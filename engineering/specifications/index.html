Material selection

6063 vs 6061 Aluminum: Why the Temper and Shape Matter

An alloy name is only one part of an aluminum pergola specification. Temper, extrusion geometry, wall thickness, tolerances, connections, finish, and verified material records all affect the final component.

6063 fits architectural extrusion priorities

The current Max Pergola design basis is extruded 6063-T6. 6063 is widely selected for architectural extrusions because it can support complex profile shapes and a consistent finished appearance. The T6 designation describes a heat-treated temper; the purchase documents should require the actual alloy and temper to be supported by the mill certificate.

That designation does not make every 6063-T6 profile equivalent. A 150 mm square column with a stated wall is not interchangeable with another profile that merely uses the same alloy. Internal webs, corners, local thickness, straightness, tolerances, holes, cuts, welds, fasteners, and connection details change performance.

6061 is not an automatic upgrade

6061 is commonly associated with structural and machined applications, but a material comparison cannot be reduced to “6061 strong, 6063 weak.” A designer evaluates the specified temper and mechanical properties together with the actual cross-section, unsupported length, bracing, connection, load path, deflection limit, durability, fabrication, and finish requirements.

Substituting alloy, temper, profile revision, or wall thickness after review can invalidate the basis of calculations and shop drawings. Any substitution should be documented, checked by the responsible parties, and reflected in the material certificate, drawings, BOM, packing list, and approval record.

Reading the BOM

Use the Bill of Materials to Control Scope

Part identity

Each major extrusion, bracket, base, drain component, louver group, drive, control, seal, fastener kit, and accessory should be traceable to the approved configuration. Part labels and drawing callouts reduce guesswork during inventory and assembly.

Configuration-specific quantity

Louver, hardware, seal, gutter, drive, and accessory quantities change with footprint, roof modules, freestanding or attached layout, and selected options. Marketing summaries explain scope; the approved packing list controls delivered quantity.

Inclusions and exclusions

A useful BOM distinguishes shipped product from field work. Anchors, concrete, reinforcement, sealants, conductors, branch circuits, lifts, unloading, labor, permits, taxes, and third-party accessories should never be assumed from a product photo.

Resolve discrepancies before assembly. Photograph packaging and labels, compare the shipment with the approved BOM and packing list, quarantine damaged or unidentified parts, and obtain a written disposition instead of substituting components in the field.

Finish and durability

Specify the Coating and Exposure Conditions

Powder-coat approval

Record the coating system, color standard, gloss range, pretreatment, cure requirements, acceptable appearance, repair method, and any requested test evidence. Approve a physical or controlled sample when color matching matters; screen images and names such as “Graphite” are not complete finish specifications.

Site environment and maintenance

Coastal salt, pool chemicals, irrigation, industrial contaminants, trapped debris, dissimilar metals, standing water, scratches, and harsh cleaners can affect durability. Confirm suitable materials and isolation details for the site, then follow the supplied cleaning, inspection, drainage, and touch-up guidance.

Specification FAQ

Questions Before Technical Approval

Do the listed profile sizes prove the pergola's wind or snow rating?

No. Capacity requires verified material properties, section properties, geometry, bracing, connections, anchors, foundation conditions, design criteria, load combinations, and appropriate calculations for the exact configuration.

Are the wall thicknesses minimum or nominal?

This page identifies them as nominal values from the current commercial profile drawing. The approved drawing, applicable tolerances, inspection method, and purchase specification should define acceptance for the actual order.

What should a complete pergola BOM include?

It should identify the configuration and major frame, roof, drainage, drive, control, seal, hardware, finish, accessory, spare-part, and documentation scope, with quantities or traceable packing references appropriate to the approved layout.

Can a contractor substitute 6061 for 6063?

Not without documented review. Alloy and temper affect fabrication, finish, material properties, and the basis of approved drawings or calculations. Any change should be accepted by the responsible parties and carried through the technical and quality records.

Which document controls if the website and quote differ?

The executed commercial agreement and its incorporated approved documents should define the order. Resolve every conflict among the quote, shop drawing, specification, BOM, packing list, engineering scope, and warranty in writing before production.
