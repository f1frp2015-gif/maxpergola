Closed-roof planning

A Rain-Ready Roof Starts Below the Louvers

Closing the blades is only the first step. Water still needs a continuous path through the perimeter channels, into the selected posts, and away from foundations and walkways.

  • Confirm the permitted roof orientation and blade direction
  • Keep channels clear during assembly and routine maintenance
  • Choose drain-post locations around doors and traffic areas
  • Route discharge away from the house foundation
  • Treat the pergola as an outdoor structure, not a sealed room

Louvered pergola FAQ

Questions to Resolve Before the Quote

What is a louvered pergola?

It is a pergola with rotating roof blades. Open louvers admit direct light and airflow; angled blades create adjustable shade; compatible closed blades shed rain into integrated drainage channels.

Are louvered pergolas waterproof?

No outdoor pergola should be described as a sealed indoor room. A compatible closed roof can provide rain protection, but wind-driven rain can enter through the open sides and severe weather can exceed normal drainage conditions.

Can I choose manual or motorized louvers?

Yes. Standard uses manual louvers; Pro and Max use motorized packages. The final quote identifies the controls, electrical preparation, and accessories included.

How long does assembly take?

The prepared structural frame can typically be assembled in about 45 minutes by an experienced two- to three-person crew after the jobsite and anchors are ready. Roof components, final anchoring, drainage, wiring, accessories, and inspections take additional time.
